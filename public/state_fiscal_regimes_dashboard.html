Not all states run on the same fiscal engine

Some Indian states pay their own way. Some lean on Delhi’s transfers to fund development. A few are stuck paying yesterday’s bills, interest on old debt and pensions, with little left to build. Every state’s budget from 1990 to 2024 is sorted into a handful of fiscal types, tracking how each state moved between them.

How to label states, without the math

The method starts from ten simple facts about each state’s budget every year: the share it raises in its own taxes, the share from central transfers and grants, how much goes to interest, pensions, social services and investment, and whether it runs a day-to-day surplus or deficit. To keep the comparison fair across three decades, each state is measured against all the other states in that same year, so nationwide events like GST or pay commissions don’t distort the picture.

A grouping method then sorts all 962 state-years into the six types shown above, based on which budgets look alike. A state’s current type is simply the one it has occupied most in its last five years. Nothing is estimated or filled in; every figure comes straight from the Reserve Bank’s state finances database.

What the components mean

Method

Data. RBI e-STATES database, accounts from 1990-91 to 2023-24. UTs excluded except NCT Delhi. Jammu and Kashmir retained since it was a state for 30 of its 34 years. State-years before a state existed are recorded as zeros in the source and were dropped, not imputed. All 962 valid state-years were complete in the source, so no values were estimated or imputed.

Features. Ten scale free shares per state-year: own tax, share in central taxes, own non tax and central grants as shares of revenue receipts; social services, economic services, interest payments and pensions as shares of revenue expenditure; capital outlay over combined revenue expenditure and capital outlay; and the revenue balance as a share of revenue receipts.

Year demeaning. Each feature is expressed as a deviation from that year's cross state mean before analysis. This removes common era effects such as GST, pay commissions and Finance Commission cycles, so a state-year is characterised by how it differed from its contemporaries rather than which decade it sits in. Without this step, states with short recent histories are compared against other eras instead of other states.

Temporal PCA. Demeaned features are z-scored and decomposed jointly across all 962 state-years. The first four components carry 75.6 percent of variance and define the space in which regimes are identified.

Regimes. K-means on the first four PC scores clusters state-years, not states, into six fiscal regimes, chosen on silhouette score and interpretability. Single year flickers are removed with a three year rolling mode filter, which changed 25 of 962 labels. A state's current type is the modal regime of its last five years and its full regime history is retained, so a type is always traceable to when the state entered it.

Structural zeros. Jammu and Kashmir receives no share in central taxes after becoming a UT in 2019-20 and NCT Delhi receives none throughout. These are true zeros and were retained.

Source: RBI e-STATES database, accounts 1990-91 to 2023-24@indiaemerges
